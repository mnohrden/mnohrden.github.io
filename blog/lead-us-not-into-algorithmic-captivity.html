Lead us not into algorithmic captivity, but deliver us from wanting it.

June 1, 2026

I went to a Lenten talk on freedom given by a priest at St. Mary Cathedral in Austin. During the talk, he outlined an analogy for freedom that gave me words to describe an intuition I’ve held for a period of time, but couldn’t express: Freedom is like a child learning to play the piano. When the child begins, she can play any note on the piano; she is free to make any sounds she pleases, but she does not have freedom of agency to play, say, Beethoven’s 3rd Movement of the Moonlight Sonata. Her teacher kindly gives her sheet music, exercises, and guidance to learn to play how she wants. She can always play jazz, pop, or go back to banging on random keys. And these are all perfectly valid options, some just produce better results than others. If she has a goal she is striving for (i.e., learning the piece) the only way she can really achieve it is by striving for the goal. While trying to achieve that goal she will end up following the rules, doing exercises, and maybe have a strict practice schedule.

In our own lives, rules and obligations that are properly aligned with our goals start to feel less like rules and more like voluntary actions. Technology has given us a piano to play with thousands of keys. The current path has already ushered in the age of post-scarcity, and in time it will strip away every external constraint. [1] In time, no one will have to do anything.  When external constraints vanish, the only remaining constraint is internal. What we do collapses to what we want, so the remaining question is the quality of our wants.

I’m not here to tell you about how bad Instagram Reels are for your dopamine pathways or something like that. I think that horse has been beaten to death at this point. You know the deal. So let’s just talk about what is.

The accessibility or features presented by technology are not the root of the problems our society faces. If it was, then the whole world should just be Amish. And I’ve seen a trend of “everything post-industrial revolution is prima facie bad and we need to revert”. But how free are the Amish? It’s no coincidence that they only have a presence in the US and Canada. This sort of disconnection from the real world can only exist in the bubble of countries that are kind enough to tolerate it. I think the pacifism stuff is respectable, but it’s also the reason there were never any Amish in the Soviet Union. In the Amish example you can see that abstaining from tech doesn’t make us free, because tech isn’t what made us unfree.

The point is, proper technology integration, while being surrounded by technology, is actually the ideal. Now, that’s not to say that there aren’t forces actively working against us in this space. Technology gives others the ability to monetize your attention in an unnatural way. Targeted advertising aligns profit with attention capture. Everyone wants your attention, even people you don’t know. 

The more freedom is handed to us by technology, the more valuable the product your attention becomes to those interested in capturing it.

Wireheading is already here, you don’t need brain chips or simulation pods. You basically need an iPhone X. A good enough simulation can fill in the rest of the gaps to make the content sufficiently addictive to prevent you from doing anything else in your free time. The end goal of AI research at Meta is less about artificial intelligence and more about simulated intelligence. It’s not difficult to see how it would be much more profitable for Meta or Google to simulate your brain than actual superintelligence if they want to maximally extract value from you. And capture your attention. Note that it’s not in Meta’s interest to make you scroll on your phone every second of the day. You still need to have a job so that your attention is still worth something to Meta. You don’t think they added in screen time features for your mental health, do you? Meta’s number one customer is someone who has a job, is depressed (they spend all their free time scrolling), but not too depressed to stop living (users committing suicide means less profit). I frequently pray that I will not be captivated by the bliss of wireheading.

The cure to wireheading is simple, but is constantly becoming more difficult to implement. It’s love [2]. Unconditional, selfless love. Unconditional, selfless love is the cure because its object is someone outside ourselves. A simulation could counterfeit the feeling of being loved, but that’s exactly the trap of simulation. What it can’t do is be something real for you to love that is truly affected. It’s the one reward reality is required for, and why escaping the loop and loving are the same act.

Now this might sound pretty lofty, and it is, but if you focus on this above all else, you truly can be delivered from the desire to be stimulated by a dopaminergically maximized video feed. Real love has a cost and a giving of oneself that pulls us out of the self-centered slothfulness of a simulated world. Technology usage with love as the aim does not eliminate the technology. It moderates the use to be directed at something more valuable. This type of love pulls us beyond an attention-for-dopamine transaction to freedom and true living. Sufficient alignment to selfless love paves the future for us. This is the music we’re striving to play.

Voluntary discipline doesn’t stop with technology use. Your own path to mastery looks different from mine, and I hope you can find it. (I wish I could give advice). We can’t learn to play in this vast world by ourselves. We have many sources of guidelines that direct us on a path towards increasing love in our lives. We have to align with that transcendental love so that we are repulsed by that which seeks to take us away from that love. [3] The sheet music (guidelines for how to live properly) keeps us on track towards our goal of loving more.

Today’s technology gives us freedom that people just a few thousand years ago would believe were signs of gods. Get the latest MacBook, and upgrade your Claude subscription. Seriously! You’re going to need these tools to play the best music you can, but you need to use them properly. The key is to pray that you are delivered from desiring anything but the most beautiful music.

  2. When I use the word love, I mean this kind of love.

  3.  I have experienced this firsthand so that’s why I’m writing all this. It’s difficult to see that this is actually true without trying it out, so I encourage you to do so if you feel so compelled.
